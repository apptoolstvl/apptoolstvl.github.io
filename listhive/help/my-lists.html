My Lists

My Lists

A list gathers everything for one purpose: this week's dinners, a birthday party, a class plan, a reading list, a set of bookmarks. You build it from whatever it needs: notes and headings, photos, videos, links, files, voice notes, scanned text, things shared from other apps, and items from your libraries.

Create a list

  1. On the My Lists tab, tap +.
  2. Type a name such as Holiday Baking, and a description if you like.
  3. Choose the MODE: Simple (Flat list of items) or Groups (Items organised in groups). See below.
  4. For a list with groups, choose how groups are named under IDENTIFY GROUPS BY: Date (e.g. 2026-10-04) or Custom Label (e.g. Weekend · Slow and Sweet).
  5. Tap Create List. The list starts empty: add whatever it needs.

Simple lists and lists with groups

🎂 Sam's Birthday Dinner is a Simple list: one evening, one list of things. Tapping it goes straight to its items.

🍲 This Week's Meals uses Groups: Dinners · Mon to Fri and Weekend · Slow and Sweet. Tapping it shows the groups first, each with its own items and progress. Use groups when a list repeats: a weekly plan, a class that meets many times, a set list per gig.

You can switch a list between the two later. Long-press the list on the My Lists tab and choose Edit. A list can only become Simple when it has one group left.

Working with groups

Inside a list with groups you'll see each group's name, item count, notes and a small progress circle that fills as items are marked done.

  • Tap + to add a New Group. Give it a date or label and optional Notes, such as Sam's birthday Sunday.
  • Sort groups by Name, Created or Modified. Tap the same button again to reverse the order.
  • Long-press a group to Edit Name, Duplicate Group (handy for next week's plan) or Delete Group.
  • Tap ☆ to add a group to your favourites.

Open a group to see its items. Section headings such as MONDAY break it into parts, notes sit between the dishes, and a green tick marks items that are done.

The buttons along the top are:

  • ≡: reorder mode.
  • ▤: field visibility.
  • Share: see Sharing.
  • •••: Reset Progress, which clears every tick in the group.

Tap any item to open it in the player.

All the ways to add to a list

Tap + inside a list (or inside a group) to open Add to List. Pick whatever suits, and mix them freely in the same list:

NoteA few lines of text on a coloured tile (more)
Section DividerA heading that splits the list into parts (more)

Scan TextThe words from a photo of a page or card (more)

You can also fill a list from outside the app: share a link, text or photo from your browser, gallery or any app into ListHive (more), import a .listhive file someone sent you, or scan a QR code.

Sam's Birthday Dinner shows how varied a list can be. Most of it was added straight into the list:

  • a File, Shopping list.pdf;
  • an Audio Note, Reminder from the market;
  • a URL, Birthday cake ideas;
  • a Video of the pasta water;
  • coloured Notes and Section headings (BEFORE THE DAY, STARTER, MAIN, CAKE);
  • a Scan of Grandma's recipe card and a Photo of the cake.

Only the Greek Salad and the pasta come from the Recipe Box library.

Notes and section headings

  • Note: tap + → Note, type, pick a tile colour (yellow, green, blue, pink, lavender or peach) and tap Add Note. The scan icon in the note box can fill it from a photo of some text.
  • Section Divider: tap + → Section Divider, type a heading such as MONDAY or CAKE, and tap Add Section. Sections split a long list into parts, aren't counted as items, and never need ticking.

Notes count as items, so you can tick them off too, which is handy for reminders like Garlic into the oil at 19:25. To change a note later, long-press it and choose Edit.

Photos, videos, files and links

  • Photo and Video: choose Camera, the Photo Library / Video Library, or Browse Files, then add a description. Photos open full screen with pinch-to-zoom; videos play right in the list.
  • File: any document, such as a PDF shopping list, a spreadsheet or a ticket. Tap it later to open it in another app.
  • URL: paste a web address and give it a title, e.g. Birthday cake ideas. Tap Open Link to visit it.

ListHive keeps its own copy of photos, videos and files, so they keep working even if you delete the originals. To change a description later, long-press the item and choose Edit Description (or Edit for a link).

Audio notes

An audio note records your voice and, if Live transcription is on, writes down what you say as you speak. The Reminder from the market note in Sam's Birthday Dinner was made this way.

  1. Tap + → Audio Note.
  2. Check the Language, then tap Start Recording. You can pause and carry on.
  3. When you stop, add a description, correct the transcript if needed, and save.

ListHive asks for microphone permission the first time. Live transcription needs Android 13 or later. On older phones you record first, and ListHive offers to transcribe the recording afterwards.

Scan text from a photo

Scan Text reads the words in a photo, such as a printed page, a label or a handwritten recipe card, and turns them into text you can search and copy.

  1. Tap + → Scan Text.
  2. Choose the Language / script (Latin covers English and most European languages).
  3. Tap Choose an Image to take a photo or pick one.
  4. Check the Scanned text, then choose Save Image + Text, Image Only or Text Note Only.

The first scan in each script downloads a small language pack, so you need to be online the first time. If you see “language pack is still downloading”, wait a minute and tap Rescan.

Content shared from other apps

Often the quickest way to fill a list is from the app you're already in. In your browser, gallery, YouTube or almost any app, tap Share and choose ListHive. Then pick Add Link, Add Note or Add Media, choose the list (and group) and tap Save. You can even create a new list on the spot.

Items from a library

If you keep reusable things in a library, you can drop them into any list without typing them again. Library items show their summary fields (Italian · 15 · Easy) and which library they came from. Edit the item in its library and every list that uses it sees the change.

  1. Tap + → Library Item to open Add Items from Library.
  2. Check the library next to From:. Tap it to switch.
  3. Search if you need to. @field: text works here too, and the chips sort the list.
  4. Tick the items you want. Items already here are marked Already added.
  5. Tap Add 2 Items (the number matches your selection).

The ⓘ button previews an item, and the clock shows where it has been used before. That's useful for avoiding the same dinner two weeks running.

You can also go the other way: open an item in its library and tap Add to My List.

Local entries

Sometimes a list needs something shaped like a library item that isn't worth keeping in the library, like a one-off Friday Pizza Night. A local entry uses the library's fields but lives only in this list.

  1. In Add Items from Library, tap New Local Entry (using library structure).
  2. Fill in the fields, exactly as for a library item.
  3. Tap Add.

Local entries are marked Local entry in the list. If it turns out to be a keeper, open it and tap Save to Library to make it a normal library item.

Reorder, move, copy and remove

Long-press any item for its menu:

  • Edit / Edit Description: for notes, photos, videos, files and links.
  • Select: start selecting several items. The bar at the bottom then offers Share, Move, Copy and Remove.
  • Toggle Done: tick or untick without opening the player.
  • Duplicate: an extra copy in the same place.
  • Move to… / Copy to…: send it to another group or list.
  • Remove from group: takes it out of this list only. A library item stays in its library.

To change the order, tap ≡ at the top, then long-press and drag items. Tap Done to save.

Choose which fields show

For library items and local entries, tap ▤ at the top of a list or group to decide, field by field:

  • View: whether the field is shown when you play the list;
  • Share: whether it's included when you share the list as text or a picture.

For example, hide Method when sharing a menu with guests, but keep it in view for yourself. The icon turns orange when a group has its own settings.

Search all your lists

The search box at the top of the My Lists tab looks through every list, group and item at once. Type at least two letters and results appear under Lists, Groups and Items. Tap a result to jump straight to it.
