Getting started with ListHive

ListHive keeps the things you use again and again (recipes, songs, exercises, contacts) in libraries, then lets you pick from them into lists for a particular week, event or session. This guide follows one kitchen from start to finish.

The two ideas behind ListHive

Library

Your master collection. You decide which details each item has: for recipes that might be the cuisine, cooking time, a photo, the ingredients and the method. Add a recipe once and keep it forever.

My Lists

Selections picked from your libraries for a purpose: this week's dinners, a birthday party, a class plan. Lists can hold notes, photos, links and voice notes too, and you can tick items off as you go.

The first time you open ListHive, the How ListHive Works guide explains the same two ideas. You can bring it back at any time with the ? button at the top of the My Lists screen.

The example used in this guide

Every page uses the same kitchen, so you can see how the pieces fit together:

  • Recipe Box: a library of nine recipes, each with a cuisine, cooking time, difficulty, photo, ingredients, method, a photo gallery, tags, a source link and a how-to video.
  • Kitchen Suppliers: a library of shops and market stalls with phone numbers, email addresses, map locations, a price list and a voice memo.
  • 🍲 This Week's Meals: a list with two groups, Dinners · Mon to Fri and Weekend · Slow and Sweet, pulled from the Recipe Box.
  • 🎂 Sam's Birthday Dinner: a simple list for one evening that mixes recipes with a shopping list, a voice note, a photographed recipe card and a video.
Try it yourself

You can download the same example and import it into ListHive to follow along: Recipe-Box.listhive (10 MB), then Birthday-Dinner.listhive (9 MB). See Importing a .listhive file.

Your first list in five minutes

  1. Create a library. Open the Libraries tab and tap +. Name it, for example Recipes, and add a few fields such as Recipe, Cuisine and Time (min). Choose one field as the primary field: it is the name each item is known by. More about creating libraries.
  2. Add some items. Open the library and tap Add Item. Fill in a recipe and tap Save. Repeat for a few more, or import many at once from a spreadsheet with CSV import.
  3. Create a list. Open the My Lists tab, tap +, give the list a name such as This Week's Meals and choose Simple or Groups. More about lists.
  4. Add items from your library. Inside the list (or inside a group), tap +, choose Library Item and tick the recipes you want.
  5. Use it. Tap any item to open it full screen, swipe between items, and tap Mark Done as you finish each one. More about playing a list.

Finding your way around

The bar at the bottom of the screen has four buttons:

  • My Lists: your lists, with search across every list, group and item.
  • Libraries: your libraries and their items.
  • Settings: import, backup, appearance, security and help.
  • Faves: opens your favourites. Tap the ☆ on any list, group or library to add it.

Inside a list or library, the row under the title shows where you are, for example Home › Libraries › Recipe Box. Tap any part of it to jump back.

No account needed

ListHive works offline and keeps everything on your device. There is no sign-in and no cloud sync, so remember to back up now and then.
